My research develops AI-driven neural interfaces that decode motor intent from muscle and brain signals (HD-sEMG and EEG) to enable intuitive, generalizable control of wearable robotic systems for human augmentation and rehabilitation. By integrating machine learning, physiological sensing, neuromusculoskeletal modeling, and mechatronics, I build systems that interpret motor intent, reveal the mechanisms underlying motor control, and advance next-generation neural–machine interfaces and assistive technologies.

Wearable robots hold significant promise for rehabilitation, human augmentation, and mobility assistance, yet their widespread adoption remains constrained by limitations in adaptability, usability, and seamless human–robot interaction. My research addresses these challenges by bridging biological and machine intelligence through three interconnected thrusts. *First, I design biomechanically informed wearable robotic systems and human-centered control strategies for assistance and rehabilitation.* *Second, I study human motor control through neuromusculoskeletal modeling and deep reinforcement learning to clarify the interplay among neural commands, muscle dynamics, and movement biomechanics.* *Third, I develop generalizable neural decoding methods that translate neurophysiological signals, such as HD-sEMG and EEG, into estimates of motor intent and locomotor behavior.* Together, these efforts form a *Human Movement Intelligence* framework that unites neural decoding, computational motor control, and wearable robotics to create adaptive, personalized, and deployable human-centered assistive and rehabilitative technologies.
